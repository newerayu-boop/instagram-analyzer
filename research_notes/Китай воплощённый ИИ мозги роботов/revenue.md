# Where the money is in embodied AI / humanoid robotics (China focus, global comparison) — as of Nov 2026

Research note date: 2026-11-09. All CNY = RMB yuan. "F" = forecast, "A" = actual/reported. Many figures come from secondary write-ups of bank notes / prospectuses; primary documents (SSE prospectus, HKEX filings, bank reports) were not directly accessible — treat bank numbers as "as reported by".
Note on method: Firecrawl was unavailable (invalid key); research done via WebSearch/WebFetch, ~16 calls. Some searches returned search-engine summaries; key numbers were cross-checked against at least the cited page where possible.

## 1. Market size, shipments and unit prices (2025 A, 2026 A/F, 2030 F)

### Takeaway
Humanoids are still a sub-$1–2B/yr hardware market globally (IDC: $740M in H1 2026 on ~25k units), ~85–95% Chinese by units; 2025 shipments were ~13–17k units depending on the counter. Forecasts diverge by 2–3x (2030 global: GGII ~340k units/CNY 64bn vs Goldman 890k units; China 2030: Morgan Stanley 446k units/$15bn). Average selling prices are collapsing fast (Unitree humanoid ASP −72% in two years), so unit growth ≠ revenue growth.

## 2. Revenue models and margins by layer

### Takeaway
Today the only layers with proven, disclosed profits are (1) low-cost hardware sold mostly to research/education buyers (Unitree ~60% GM, profitable) and (2) mature component suppliers (e.g., Leaderdrive harmonic reducers ~37% GM, 22% net margin, but small and margin-compressing). The "brain" layer (foundation models, data, sim) has huge valuations but little disclosed revenue; data collection is already commoditizing (teleop CNY 500–1,000/h; portable capture CNY 100–200/h) and rental has crashed >70%.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Robot training data**
- Real-robot teleoperation: market quote CNY 500–1,000 per hour; teleop rig >CNY 200k, robot body CNY 600–700k; operator yields only 2–3 effective hours/day; real-robot collection otherwise CNY 200–500/h; portable/no-body capture CNY 100–200/h — 《具身数据发展报告2026》 (CIA dataset committee + MIIT 5th Institute), [The Paper, 2026-10-26](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34333420)
- Dataset/licensing price list (same report): standard datasets CNY 10k–500k per set; exclusive industry licences CNY 500k–5M; enterprise data subscriptions CNY 50k–500k/yr; teleop kits CNY 20k–200k/set; collection workstations CNY 100k–500k; SaaS CNY 10k–200k/yr; annotation CNY 5–100 per action/trajectory, CNY 0.5–5 per language instruction — [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34333420)
- Report's diagnosis: capacity growth outpaces paid demand; most deliveries are one-off projects, hard to resell; no common OS so cross-embodiment migration costly; free open-source datasets suppress purchases; platform commission model not formed; head robot makers build collection in-house — [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34333420)

### Inferences
- Data: the per-hour collection business looks like a labor-arbitrage service heading to commodity margins (collector pay CNY 8/h vs selling CNY 100–1,000/h now, but capacity is outrunning demand). The defensible part is curated/licensable datasets with resale (GuangLun's >10x resale) and outcome-linked delivery — not raw hours.
- Brain: the money in 2026 is in fundraising, not revenue — Skild's ~$30M (maybe ~$100M ARR) against $14bn valuation is the best-disclosed case globally; nothing comparable disclosed in China.
- Components: margins compress as volume rises (Leaderdrive), and leading OEMs (Unitree) self-make most parts — component profit pool is real but shrinking per unit.

### Gaps
- No disclosed revenue for Nvidia Isaac/Cosmos/Thor robotics, Horizon's robotics unit, or Chinese embodied-model licensing.
- No price per trajectory benchmark beyond annotation (CNY 5–100/item); no Scale-AI-for-robotics revenue figures found.
- Dexterous-hand and sensor supplier margins (Inspire, Zhaowei, Sanhua, Tuopu) not retrieved.

Its 2026 profit decline shows that model is exposed to the same price war seen in rental.

## 4. Where analysts see the biggest profit pools in 3–5 years

### Takeaway
Banks frame the long-run pool as overwhelmingly hardware (MS: ~$4.7tn of $5tn in 2050 is hardware), with actuators/joints the largest BOM slice; Chinese industry analysts see value migrating from "selling hours" of data to standards, licensed datasets and outcome-guaranteed deployments. Near-term (2027–2030) revenue shifts from research/education to industrial/logistics as full-size share rises (MS: 30%→70% of shipments by 2028).

### Cited Findings
- MS 2050: hardware ~$4.7tn of ~$5tn; software/data/services remainder; ~90% of installed base in industrial/commercial work — [Humanoid.guide](https://humanoid.guide/morgan-stanley-about-humanoid-robots/)

### Inferences
- 3–5 year view (my synthesis): (1) Hardware OEMs with cost leadership (Unitree model) keep generating profit, but margins likely fall from ~60% toward 30–40% as ASPs drop; (2) the "brain" layer's money will likely come via bundling into deployed robots (RaaS/per-robot licence, Skild-style) rather than standalone API sales — no evidence yet of a standalone model API market in China; (3) data collection services will commoditize; curated datasets, sim/synthetic platforms and evaluation/standards hold the margin; (4) state/education demand (55% of tenders) is the current floor but is subsidy-driven and may not scale.
- Consensus view that "brain" captures outsized value is a valuation thesis, not yet a revenue fact as of Nov 2026.
